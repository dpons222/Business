# Research

## Market Notes

Local restaurants do not all need the same service. One restaurant may need a cleaner private dining inquiry flow. Another may need ordering links fixed, Google Business Profile links cleaned up, review responses systemized, weekly specials turned into reusable content, or lapsed guests reactivated.

The opportunity is to start with a visible customer journey audit and recommend one focused improvement that is practical for that specific restaurant.

## Likely Pain Points

- Catering or private dining details are buried under a generic contact page.
- Menus are available only as PDFs that are awkward on mobile.
- Reservation, order, and contact links compete with each other.
- Google Business Profile links are outdated, generic, or hard to track.
- Online ordering has too many clicks or unclear handoff between platforms.
- Instagram traffic points to a generic homepage instead of a specific offer.
- Seasonal events, brunches, tasting menus, gift cards, or holiday meals do not have focused promotion.
- Reviews receive inconsistent or generic responses.
- Weekly specials are posted manually and not reused across channels.
- Email/SMS lists exist but are underused.
- Event and private dining inquiries are not tracked through a simple pipeline.

## Strong Restaurant Segments

- Restaurants with catering menus, party trays, or office lunch potential.
- Restaurants with private rooms, event spaces, patios, or large-party offerings.
- Restaurants with premium dinner, brunch, holiday, tasting-menu, or event moments.
- Bakeries, cafes, and casual restaurants with group orders or repeat-purchase potential.
- Restaurants with active social channels but weak website or link conversion paths.
- Restaurants with many reviews but inconsistent owner response workflows.

## Weak Segments

- Very small restaurants with no web presence and no clear contact path.
- Restaurants that only use marketplace delivery platforms and do not want direct traffic.
- Chains with locked marketing systems.
- Businesses with poor public information, no menu, or no current offer to improve.
- Restaurants where the likely fix requires custom POS, delivery, or reservation platform work before a pilot can start.

## Competitive Alternatives

Restaurants may already use:

- Website builders.
- Restaurant website agencies.
- Reservation platforms.
- Online ordering platforms.
- POS marketing tools.
- Email/SMS platforms.
- Social media agencies.
- Link-in-bio tools.
- Google Business Profile posts.
- Freelancers for one-off web edits.

The experiment should avoid competing as a broad agency. The sharper angle is a diagnostic recommendation plus one focused implementation module.

## Channel Hypotheses

Likely outreach channels:

- Direct email to owner, manager, events, catering, or marketing contact.
- Contact form when email is unavailable.
- Instagram DM only after a public business contact path is unclear.
- Local restaurant association lists, chamber directories, Google Maps, and local press lists.

Likely pilot traffic or workflow sources:

- Google Business Profile.
- Instagram profile link or story link.
- Email newsletter.
- SMS list.
- Existing website button.
- QR card or table tent.
- Review platforms.
- Existing reservation or order links.

## Research Questions For First Batch

- Which diagnostic category appears most often?
- Which recommendation type gets the strongest response?
- Do owners respond better to an audit-first message or a module-specific message?
- What objections come up first: budget, timing, trust, platform fit, or lack of perceived need?
- Does one restaurant category produce stronger prospects?
