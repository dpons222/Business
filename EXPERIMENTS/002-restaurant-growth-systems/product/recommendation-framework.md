# Recommendation Framework

## Goal

Pick the most useful first improvement for each restaurant instead of forcing every prospect into the same service.

## Diagnostic Flow

1. Identify the restaurant's public customer journey.
2. Check the highest-value actions the restaurant appears to support.
3. Look for visible friction, confusion, outdated information, or missed follow-up.
4. Choose one recommendation category.
5. Write a one-sentence recommendation.
6. Create a light example only if it can be done quickly.

## Public Customer Journey Areas

Review:

- Google Business Profile.
- Website homepage.
- Menu page or PDF.
- Reservation path.
- Online ordering path.
- Catering or private dining page.
- Contact form or email path.
- Instagram profile links.
- Review profiles and response patterns.
- Events, specials, gift cards, loyalty, or newsletter paths.

## Recommendation Categories

```text
catering_private_dining
email_sms_promo
gbp_menu_order_cleanup
online_ordering_audit
event_lead_tracking
review_response_workflow
weekly_specials_content
birthday_loyalty_reactivation
website_menu_cleanup
landing_campaign_page
```

## Selection Rules

Choose `catering_private_dining` when:

- the restaurant offers groups, events, catering, private rooms, or party trays,
- the current inquiry path is buried or unclear,
- the likely order value is higher than a standard table.

Choose `email_sms_promo` when:

- the restaurant has repeat demand, specials, events, or loyalty potential,
- there is evidence of a list or promo channel,
- the owner needs repeatable communication more than a new page.

Choose `gbp_menu_order_cleanup` when:

- public links are inconsistent,
- menu/order/reservation links are outdated or confusing,
- the fastest improvement is link clarity.

Choose `online_ordering_audit` when:

- takeout or delivery appears important,
- ordering takes too many steps,
- buttons or handoffs between platforms are confusing.

Choose `event_lead_tracking` when:

- private dining or events exist,
- inquiries may be valuable,
- the restaurant needs a simple status/follow-up process.

Choose `review_response_workflow` when:

- the restaurant has active reviews,
- owner responses are missing, inconsistent, or generic,
- reputation follow-up is easier than changing the site.

Choose `weekly_specials_content` when:

- specials are posted inconsistently,
- the restaurant has recurring offers,
- the same content could be reused across Google, Instagram, email, or the website.

Choose `birthday_loyalty_reactivation` when:

- the restaurant has repeat-customer potential,
- email/SMS or loyalty tools are visible or likely,
- the need is bringing guests back rather than converting first-time visitors.

Choose `website_menu_cleanup` when:

- the site is current enough to improve but messy,
- menu, hours, location, or calls to action are hard to scan,
- a narrow cleanup is more useful than a new campaign.

Choose `landing_campaign_page` when:

- one offer needs a clean destination,
- the restaurant has a specific campaign, event, catering offer, or seasonal push,
- traffic can be sent from Google, Instagram, email, QR cards, or the current website.

## Recommendation Format

Use this structure:

```text
Observed: [specific public issue or opportunity].
Recommendation: [one focused improvement].
Why it fits: [restaurant-specific reason].
First deliverable: [one module].
What to track: [one to three signals].
```

## Demo Rules

- Create a visual demo only if the recommendation benefits from one.
- For workflow-heavy modules, use a simple process diagram, tracker sample, or copy sequence instead of a web page.
- Keep unpaid examples light.
- Do not imply the restaurant approved the recommendation.
- Do not fabricate menu items, offers, reviews, capacities, policies, or performance results.
