# Research

## Market Notes

Local restaurants already spend time and money trying to turn attention into reservations, orders, catering requests, group bookings, event attendance, and repeat visits. Many owners rely on a mix of website pages, Google Business Profile, Instagram, reservation widgets, online ordering platforms, printed menus, and word of mouth.

The opportunity is not to replace those systems immediately. The opportunity is to create a focused page that makes one valuable action easier to understand and easier to take.

## Likely Pain Points

- Catering or private dining details are buried under a generic contact page.
- Menus are available only as PDFs that are awkward on mobile.
- Reservation, order, and contact links compete with each other.
- Instagram traffic points to a generic homepage instead of a specific campaign.
- Seasonal events, brunches, tasting menus, or holiday meals do not have focused pages.
- Owners cannot easily tell whether a campaign link is creating calls, inquiries, or clicks.

## Strong Restaurant Segments

- Restaurants with catering menus or party trays.
- Restaurants with private rooms, event spaces, patios, or large-party offerings.
- Restaurants with premium dinner, brunch, holiday, or tasting-menu moments.
- Bakeries, cafes, and casual restaurants that sell group orders or office catering.
- Restaurants with active social channels but weak website conversion paths.

## Weak Segments

- Very small restaurants with no web presence and no clear contact path.
- Restaurants that only use marketplace delivery platforms and do not want direct traffic.
- Chains with locked marketing systems.
- Businesses with poor public information, no menu, or no current offer to promote.

## Competitive Alternatives

Restaurants may already use:

- Website builders.
- Restaurant website agencies.
- Reservation platforms.
- Online ordering platforms.
- Social media link-in-bio tools.
- Google Business Profile posts.
- Freelancers for one-off web edits.

The experiment should avoid competing as a general website vendor. The sharper angle is a focused, measurable campaign page that supports a specific promotion or high-value action.

## Channel Hypotheses

Likely outreach channels:

- Direct email to owner, manager, events, or catering contact.
- Contact form when email is unavailable.
- Instagram DM only after a public business contact path is unclear.
- Local restaurant association lists, chamber directories, and Google Maps research.

Likely traffic channels for a pilot:

- Google Business Profile link.
- Instagram profile link or story link.
- Email newsletter.
- Existing website button.
- QR card or table tent.
- Small local ad campaign only if the client already wants paid traffic.

## Research Questions For First Batch

- Which restaurant categories show the clearest high-value action?
- Are catering and private dining contacts easy to find?
- Do restaurant owners respond better to a mocked page idea, a short audit note, or a no-demo consult offer?
- What objections come up first: budget, timing, trust, platform fit, or lack of perceived need?
- Does one city or cuisine category produce stronger prospects?
