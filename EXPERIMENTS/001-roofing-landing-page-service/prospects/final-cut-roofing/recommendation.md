# Final Cut Roofing Recommendation

## Purpose

Use this recommendation to explain why Final Cut Roofing should test a focused storm damage / free roof inspection landing page.

This is not positioned as a full website replacement. It is positioned as a focused landing page for traffic from storm-season campaigns, Google Business Profile links, local ads, door-hanger QR codes, email/SMS follow-up, or sales conversations.

Recommended demo URL:

```text
/prospects/final-cut-roofing
```

## 1. Current Page Strengths

- Final Cut Roofing already has a clear free roof inspection offer.
- The current site has strong brand assets: recognizable logo, black/blue color direction, project photography, and service-focused copy.
- The page already speaks to relevant homeowner problems: hail damage, storm damage, roof inspection, emergency repairs, and insurance claim guidance.
- The company has useful proof elements to build around, including project photos, review proof, and local service-area positioning.
- The existing page gives us enough material to create a page that still feels like Final Cut Roofing, not a generic outside template.

## 2. Current Page Conversion Opportunities

- The inspection request should be easier to act on immediately above the fold.
- The page can focus more tightly on one primary action: request a free roof inspection or call.
- Storm visitors are often in a hurry, so the first screen should quickly answer:
  - What happened?
  - Who can help?
  - Is the inspection free?
  - How do I request it?
- Stronger project imagery and proof can appear closer to the call to action.
- The page can make the process feel lower-risk by explaining inspection, documentation, findings, insurance guidance, and next steps before the visitor submits.

## 3. What the Demo Changes

- Keeps Final Cut Roofing's logo, color direction, phone numbers, local market, project photos, and storm-damage offer.
- Leads with a stronger photo-driven hero section that immediately feels like Final Cut Roofing.
- Places the inspection form above the fold beside the main offer.
- Uses a focused headline around storm damage and free roof inspection.
- Adds visible trust cues near the CTA, including customer-service proof and service areas.
- Adds supporting sections for:
  - why urgent roof inspection matters after hail/wind,
  - what the inspection includes,
  - project photos,
  - process clarity,
  - insurance claim assistance,
  - review proof,
  - FAQ,
  - final call to action.
- Keeps the page focused on inspection requests instead of trying to explain every possible service in equal depth.

## 4. Why Those Changes Matter

- A clearer first screen can reduce friction for homeowners who are deciding whether to call or request an inspection.
- A visible form can capture homeowners who do not want to call immediately.
- A click-to-call CTA can still support urgent mobile visitors who want help now.
- Project photos and review proof help the page feel credible before the visitor reaches the form.
- Process and FAQ sections reduce uncertainty for homeowners who are unsure whether they have roof damage.
- The page gives Final Cut Roofing a campaign-specific destination rather than sending every visitor to a general website flow.

Do not promise guaranteed leads or revenue. The defensible claim is:

```text
This page is designed to improve the path from storm-damage interest to inspection request.
```

## 5. How They Could Test It

Recommended low-risk test:

- Keep the current website active.
- Use the new page as a focused landing page for one campaign or traffic source.
- Run the test for 30 days or through one storm-response period.
- Send qualified traffic to the page from one or more sources:
  - Google Business Profile link,
  - Google Ads,
  - Facebook or Instagram local ads,
  - door-hanger or mailer QR code,
  - email/SMS follow-up,
  - sales team follow-up link,
  - storm damage blog or service page CTA.
- Compare the page's activity against the current inspection page or previous campaign activity.

Success signal:

```text
More inspection form submissions, more click-to-call activity, or clearer evidence that storm visitors are engaging with the inspection offer.
```

## 6. What Tracking Would Show

The page should track:

- page views,
- traffic source,
- form submissions,
- click-to-call taps,
- primary CTA clicks,
- secondary CTA clicks,
- mobile vs desktop usage,
- which sections visitors reach,
- form-start vs form-submit behavior,
- simple weekly notes on edits and results.

This tracking supports the optional monthly add-on because the ongoing work is not vague maintenance. It is conversion tracking, small copy/layout improvements, and campaign learning.

## 7. Suggested Pilot Offer

Position the pilot as:

```text
A focused storm damage / free roof inspection landing page that keeps Final Cut Roofing's branding and project photos, but makes the inspection request path clearer and easier to act on.
```

Suggested price test:

```text
$1,000 setup
$300/month optional tracking, edits, and testing add-on
```

Setup includes:

- one personalized landing page,
- company branding and project imagery,
- inspection-focused copy,
- form and click-to-call CTAs,
- mobile-responsive layout,
- basic tracking plan,
- one revision pass after review.

Monthly add-on includes:

- form and click tracking review,
- minor copy/layout edits,
- CTA testing,
- traffic-source notes,
- monthly summary of what changed and what happened.

Conservative ROI framing:

```text
If the page helps create even one additional qualified roof replacement or storm repair opportunity, the test may be worth the setup cost.
```

Avoid saying:

```text
This will guarantee more leads.
```

Say:

```text
This is a focused page designed to make free inspection requests easier to complete and easier to track.
```
